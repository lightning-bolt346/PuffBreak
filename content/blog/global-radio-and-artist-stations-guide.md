<div class="pb-article">
  <div class="pb-lead">PuffBreak is not trying to become another endless music service. Its radio library is a small, international dial designed for one useful moment: choosing a sound quickly, taking a real break, and returning without getting trapped in a feed.</div>

  <h2>A radio catalogue built around feelings, not shelf space</h2>
  <p>Most music products begin with an enormous search box. That is powerful when you already know what you want, but surprisingly tiring when you have three minutes between tasks. PuffBreak begins with five practical moods—Unwind, Focus, Discover, Energy and Nostalgia—then lets you narrow the choice by genre, region, language or artist.</p>
  <p>The catalogue currently contains more than 80 music-led stations and artist frequencies. Every entry needs a reason to exist: a distinctive point of view, a region the library would otherwise miss, a dependable focus sound, or an artist people genuinely return to. The aim is range without clutter.</p>

  <h2>Global stations with an actual point of view</h2>
  <p>For broad, human-curated listening, <strong>Radio Paradise</strong> moves comfortably through rock, world music and electronic textures, while its Mellow Mix offers a softer lane. <strong>KEXP</strong> brings independent discovery and live music culture from Seattle; Los Angeles station <strong>KCRW Eclectic24</strong> blends indie, soul and electronic music; and France’s <strong>FIP</strong> family covers its signature eclectic mix alongside dedicated Jazz, Groove and Rock stations.</p>
  <p>Listeners who want low-interruption focus can start with <strong>FreeCodeCamp Radio</strong>, PuffBreak’s default for first-time visitors, or choose classical music from <strong>Classical KING FM</strong> and <strong>RNZ Concert</strong>. <strong>Radio Swiss Jazz</strong>, <strong>Radio Swiss Pop</strong>, Germany’s <strong>FluxFM</strong> and Australia’s <strong>triple j</strong> each retain the character of a real station rather than feeling like interchangeable genre playlists.</p>

  <h2>India, Pakistan and South Asian listening</h2>
  <p>The South Asian shelf is intentionally wider than one generic “Bollywood” button. <strong>Radio Mirchi</strong>, <strong>Bollywood Now</strong> and <strong>Bollywood Evergreen</strong> separate current film music from familiar older favourites, while Pakistan’s <strong>City FM 89</strong> gives Urdu and English contemporary music its own place.</p>
  <p>For a reflective pause, <strong>Bhaktisudha</strong> offers Hindi bhajans and devotional music. This is grouped as a listening intention—not treated as background “world music”—so people looking for devotional calm can find it directly.</p>

  <h2>Japan, Korea and Asian pop</h2>
  <p><strong>Japan City Pop</strong> serves the polished city-pop sound that has found a new global audience. <strong>Big B Radio K-Pop</strong> provides a dedicated Korean-pop stream, and <strong>SBS PopAsia</strong> reaches across K-pop, J-pop and other Asian pop releases. These stations can also be found through region and language filters, not only through genre labels.</p>

  <h2>Arabic music, African rhythms and Latin warmth</h2>
  <p>The Middle Eastern selection includes <strong>Hotmix Medina</strong> and <strong>Amr Diab Radio</strong>. Across Africa, <strong>Amapiano FM</strong> focuses on South Africa’s amapiano movement, Nigeria’s <strong>MajorFM</strong> carries Afrobeats, and <strong>Rap Lbeldi</strong> makes room for Moroccan and Maghrebi hip-hop.</p>
  <p>Latin and Brazilian listening includes <strong>Bossa Jazz Brasil</strong>, nostalgia-led <strong>Rádio Saudade</strong> and <strong>LOS40 Urban</strong> for Spanish-language rap, reggaeton and Latin urban music. The point is not to put entire continents behind one “global” label, but to make their distinct musical identities visible.</p>

  <h2>Hip-hop, R&amp;B, soul, lo-fi and familiar decades</h2>
  <p><strong>181.FM Old School</strong>, <strong>181.FM The Beat</strong>, New Zealand’s old-school <strong>Flava</strong> and France’s genre-fluid <strong>Radio Nova</strong> give rap, R&amp;B and soul more than a token entry. <strong>Hotmix Lo-Fi</strong> covers the quieter beat-driven focus lane, while <strong>181.FM Awesome 80s</strong> exists for listeners who want instant familiarity rather than discovery.</p>

  <h2>Artist radio without building a queue</h2>
  <p>The artist shelf is for the moment when a genre is too broad but selecting individual songs is too much work. Alongside Taylor Swift, Coldplay, BTS, Bob Marley and Arijit Singh, it now includes dream-pop and late-night favourites such as <strong>Cigarettes After Sex</strong>, <strong>Joji</strong>, <strong>Lana Del Rey</strong>, <strong>Beach House</strong>, <strong>Men I Trust</strong>, <strong>Mac DeMarco</strong>, <strong>The Marías</strong>, <strong>Stephen Sanchez</strong>, <strong>yung kai</strong> and <strong>Djo</strong>.</p>
  <p>The global pop and hip-hop dial includes <strong>Drake</strong>, <strong>Kanye West</strong>, <strong>Bad Bunny</strong>, <strong>The Weeknd</strong>, <strong>Ariana Grande</strong>, <strong>Ed Sheeran</strong>, <strong>Billie Eilish</strong>, <strong>Eminem</strong>, <strong>Shakira</strong>, <strong>BLACKPINK</strong> and <strong>KAROL G</strong>. Kanye West Radio uses a verified 24/7 direct MP3 station, avoiding the embedding restrictions and extra video data of a YouTube catalogue. The Indian shelf adds <strong>A. R. Rahman</strong>, <strong>Anirudh Ravichander</strong>, <strong>Pritam</strong>, <strong>Karan Aujla</strong> and <strong>Sidhu Moose Wala</strong> without collapsing their film, Tamil and Punjabi contexts into one label.</p>
  <p>When a dependable browser-safe 24/7 artist stream is unavailable, PuffBreak shuffles a checked catalogue through its embedded player. The play button stays inside PuffBreak. Turning a catalogue radio off mutes it while the sequence continues, so tuning back in feels like returning to a station rather than restarting a song.</p>

  <h2>Why use PuffBreak instead of a normal radio directory?</h2>
  <p>A large radio directory is excellent when searching for a specific frequency. PuffBreak is designed for a different job: reducing the effort between “I need a break” and “something good is playing.” Search works across moods, genres, languages, regions and artists; favourites remember the stations worth returning to; and the small now-playing companion can minimize or disappear when the screen needs to stay quiet.</p>
  <p>The mixer also keeps radio independent from room ambience and cigarette or fireplace crackle. You can lower a station beneath rooftop sound, turn ambience off while keeping music, or use the Silent Room with radio alone. Your most recently selected station and volume preferences stay in your browser, so the next break does not begin with setup.</p>

  <div class="pb-callout"><strong>A useful difference:</strong> PuffBreak treats music as part of a short ritual, not as another feed to optimize. The interface gives you enough discovery to find the right feeling—and an obvious way to stop browsing.</div>

  <h2>How to choose a station in under ten seconds</h2>
  <p>Open <a href="/radio">the PuffBreak radio library</a> and choose the feeling first. Use Focus for instrumental or low-interruption sound, Unwind for softer music, Energy for a lift, Discover for unfamiliar selectors, or Nostalgia for something immediately recognisable. Add a region, language, genre or artist only if the first result still feels too broad.</p>
  <p>Radio streams and YouTube availability can change because the stations and artist playlists are operated by their respective providers. PuffBreak tests the catalogue, removes broken options and provides a request route when a listener’s region, language or favourite artist is missing.</p>
