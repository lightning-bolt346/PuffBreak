  <div class="pb-lead">The wrong way to begin a three-minute break is to spend two minutes choosing what to hear. A useful break soundtrack should reduce decisions, sit at the right intensity, and let you leave when the ritual ends.</div>

  <h2>Choose the feeling before the genre</h2>
  <p>“What do I want to listen to?” is a huge question. “How should I feel when I go back?” is smaller and more useful. Start with one of five intentions: calm down, hold focus, discover something, gain energy, or return to something familiar.</p>
  <p>Genre still matters, but it comes second. Lo-fi, mellow jazz, devotional music, or soft acoustic programming can all support calm. For energy, the right answer might be hip-hop, K-pop, amapiano, Bollywood, or Latin urban. The feeling is the filter; the genre is the colour.</p>

  <h2>Match intensity to the next ten minutes</h2>
  <p>If you are returning to writing, coding, reading, or a difficult conversation, choose a station with a steady shape: fewer abrupt presenter breaks, less lyrical density, and a moderate volume. If the next task needs momentum, a more rhythmic station can work—but it should lift you, not make the break harder to leave.</p>
  <div class="pb-callout"><strong>A practical rule:</strong> if the music becomes the main event, lower it or choose a calmer frequency. The break is still the product.</div>

  <h2>Use three sound layers intentionally</h2>
  <p>PuffBreak separates live radio, room ambience, and cigarette crackle because one master volume cannot express what people actually want. Try radio as the foreground at a comfortable level, room sound just beneath it, and crackle as a quiet tactile cue. For devotional, classical, or vocal stations, reduce the ambience so it does not muddy the music.</p>
  <p>You can also turn a layer off completely. A mixer is not an invitation to use everything at once. Some breaks need only rain. Some need only a familiar song. Silent Room exists for the moments when adding sound would add pressure.</p>

  <h2>Let place and language change the texture</h2>
  <p>A global radio library should not mean one English-language playlist with flags attached. A Pakistani contemporary station, Hindi bhajans, Japanese city pop, Korean pop, Brazilian bossa, Moroccan rap, Nigerian Afrobeats, French eclectic radio, and New Zealand old-school R&amp;B each carry a different sense of place.</p>
  <p>Use region and language when you want connection or discovery. Use saved stations when you do not want to decide. Artist frequencies are best treated as an occasional familiar room, not the entire library.</p>

  <h2>Keep the break finite</h2>
  <p>The most important control is not play. It is the ability to stop. PuffBreak’s radio companion can be minimized on larger screens and dismissed on phones. The library avoids autoplay on page load because modern browsers often block it—and because surprise audio is hostile. If a previous station was enabled, PuffBreak shows that it is waiting for your first interaction, then resumes only when the browser permits it.</p>

  <h2>A 20-second selection ritual</h2>
  <ol>
    <li>Name the feeling you want after the break.</li>
    <li>Choose one suggested station or one saved favourite.</li>
    <li>Set the radio comfortably below “attention-grabbing.”</li>
    <li>Add ambience only if it improves the room.</li>
    <li>Start the ritual, close the library, and stop choosing.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>That is the point of <a href="/radio">PuffBreak Radio</a>: enough variety to feel personal, with enough restraint to remain a break.</p>
