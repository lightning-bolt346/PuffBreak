  <div class="pb-lead">A bhajan break is not about filling silence. It is about giving attention a familiar, devotional shape for a few minutes.</div>

  <h2>Begin with intention, not multitasking</h2>
  <p>Devotional music can sit quietly in a workday, but a mindful break asks for a little more respect: stop the task, choose the station deliberately, and let the listening be the only active layer for a moment.</p>

  <h2>Keep the sound field simple</h2>
  <p>Start with the radio by itself. If the room feels too bare, add a small amount of rain or outdoor ambience. Avoid stacking strong café chatter, crackle, and music together; devotional vocals lose their clarity when every layer asks to be heard.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Use Silent Room for an uncluttered listen.</li>
    <li>Try Park Bench when a little air helps.</li>
    <li>Choose chai instead of the cigarette ritual if that feels more natural.</li>
    <li>Save the station so the next break begins without searching.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Hindi and Sanskrit should be discoverable</h2>
  <p>Language filters matter because “world music” is too vague to be useful. PuffBreak places Bhaktisudha under Hindi, Sanskrit, South Asia, Bhajan, Devotional, Calm and Focus so users can reach it through the path that makes sense to them.</p>

  <h2>Devotional radio is one lane, not a stereotype</h2>
  <p>Indian listening does not collapse into one category. The same library holds current Bollywood, evergreen Hindi film music, Arijit Singh, indie singer-songwriter music and devotional programming. Each deserves its own description and mood rather than a generic “India” card.</p>
